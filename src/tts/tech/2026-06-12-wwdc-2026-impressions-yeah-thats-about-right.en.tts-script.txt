Apple's WWDC 2026 wrapped up in mid-June, and if you want the mood of the whole thing in one phrase, it's this: measured expectations, mostly met. Nothing that made anyone gasp — but everything Apple announced is real, and it's actually shipping. Three moves matter most. Siri got torn down and rebuilt. That controversial glass-y interface finally got a fix. And Intel Macs got their official goodbye. Let's get into it.

Start with the big one — Siri. Because this isn't a tune-up, it's a ground-up rebuild. The new Siri is a standalone app now, and it has real reach into your world: your emails, your messages, your photos, your calendar, your files. Think of it less as a voice shortcut for web searches and more as a genuine personal context engine — something that actually knows what's going on in your life.

Here's the detail that raised eyebrows. The brains behind it? Google Gemini. Apple is running Gemini through its Private Cloud Compute setup. And Apple's promise is very specific: your personal data is not being used to train Gemini, and the inference happens in an isolated environment. So Google's model, Apple's walls around it.

Now, the catch — actually, a few catches. At launch, Siri is waitlisted, so not everyone gets it on day one. It's not available in the EU at all when iOS 27 ships, thanks to complications with the Digital Markets Act. And — this is the one developers should circle — there's no third-party API. Apple poured enormous resources into rebuilding Siri, but apps can't deeply plug into that new Siri intelligence yet. That integration surface just isn't open. So if you're building on Apple's platforms hoping to hook into this thing, the honest answer is: that's probably a WWDC 2027 story.

Next up, macOS 27 — codename Golden Gate. And this is the end of an era. Golden Gate is the last version of macOS that supports Intel hardware. After this, it's Apple Silicon only. If you're running an Apple Silicon Mac from 2020 onward — M1 or later — you're fine. If you're still on an Intel MacBook Pro, nobody's forcing you to upgrade today, but understand: you are now standing on the last safety net. On the feature side, you get Visual Intelligence baked into Spotlight, so you can search using what's actually on your screen, plus a unified toolbar, edge-to-edge sidebars, faster AirDrop, quicker network file browsing, and better Messages sync.

Then there's iOS 27, and this is where Apple quietly admitted it overreached. Last year's iOS 26 introduced that translucent, fluid "Liquid Glass" design — and the readability complaints were loud. People genuinely struggled to read text against all that transparency. So iOS 27 ships with an opacity slider. You can dial the transparency anywhere from fully solid all the way back to the original glass look. It's not a redesign, it's a pragmatic fix — and honestly, a smart one. Beyond that: custom AirPods EQ per device, and much more granular parental controls, down to the individual app and website. It runs on iPhone 11 and later.

On the Apple Intelligence front, a couple of things worth knowing. Image Playground can now generate photorealistic images — before it was stuck in stylized illustration and animation modes, now it does the real-looking stuff. And the Photos app picks up some genuinely useful editing tools. There's Extend, which stretches an image's edges outward to fill more space — very much in the spirit of Adobe's Generative Fill. There's Reframe, for adjusting composition and cropping and filling in empty areas. And Clean Up got smarter at detecting objects you want removed. Over in the Home app, you can now search your security camera footage using plain language — something like "the person on the driveway yesterday between ten and eleven in the morning" — plus 4K iCloud video storage.

So what's the takeaway if you build things for a living? The framing from the community was "Yeah, that's about right" — solid, unsurprising, and you can feel Apple playing catch-up in AI with these waitlist-gated features. And look at what's conspicuously missing: no open Siri integration API, no unified cross-device development framework, no Vision Pro updates, and no EU-compatible Siri at launch.

Let me leave you with three things to hold onto. One: Siri's rebuilt and it's powerful, running on Google Gemini through Apple's privacy architecture — but it's gated, region-limited, and closed to developers, so it's more promise than platform right now. Two: the opacity slider in iOS 27 is Apple listening to real complaints and shipping a real fix. And three — the operationally significant one — macOS is now Apple Silicon only. If you've still got CI or build servers running on Intel Mac hardware, the clock is officially ticking. Golden Gate is your last stop. Plan the migration now, not later.
