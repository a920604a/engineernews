## TL;DR
The relationship between love and contribution shouldn't be oversimplified to "only those who contribute are worthy of love".

## What and Why Now
The relationship between love and contribution is a complex issue that spans psychology, philosophy, and sociology. As society evolves, this topic is gaining more attention. Previous alternatives often equated love with contribution, implying that only those who contribute are deserving of love. However, this mindset can lead to feelings of inadequacy and rejection.

## How it Works
The relationship between love and contribution can be described by the following flowchart:

```mermaid
graph LR
    A[Love] -->|"Influences"| B[Self-Worth]
    B -->|"Influences"| C[Self-Esteem]
    C -->|"Influences"| D[Interpersonal Relationships]
    D -->|"Influences"| E[Societal Recognition]
```

In this flowchart, love can impact one's self-worth, which in turn affects self-esteem, interpersonal relationships, and societal recognition. Contribution can be seen as an expression of love, demonstrating care and appreciation for others.

## Difference from "Self-Worth"
The relationship between love and contribution differs from self-worth. Self-worth refers to an individual's self-evaluation, whereas the relationship between love and contribution concerns how people perceive others' value. While self-worth can be enhanced through contribution, this doesn't mean that only those who contribute are worthy of love.

## Real-World Implications
In practice, equating love with contribution can have negative consequences. For example, someone who doesn't contribute may feel inadequate and unaccepted. In contrast, when someone is loved and respected, they may feel confident and accepted. Therefore, we must abandon the idea that love is conditional on contribution and instead foster healthy, equal relationships.
