【Ten-Minute Psychology】Is Your Kindness Punishing Yourself? Practice Gently Recalling Your "Other-Oriented" Tendencies

## TL;DR
Kind people are often controlled by their "other-oriented" tendencies, leading to self-destruction and self-punishment.

## What is it
"Other-orientation" is a psychological state where an individual excessively focuses on others' thoughts, feelings, and evaluations, even prioritizing others' needs and expectations over their own. This state causes people to lose themselves, ignore their own needs and feelings, and ultimately leads to self-destruction and self-punishment.

## Why is it important
"Other-orientation" is a common psychological pattern that makes people lose themselves, ignore their own needs and feelings, and leads to a series of negative emotions and behaviors, such as self-punishment, self-destruction, and decreased self-confidence. Therefore, understanding and changing "other-orientation" is crucial for an individual's mental health and self-growth.

## How it works
"Other-orientation" is a deep-seated psychological pattern that usually forms in childhood. As individuals grow, they continuously learn and form their self-awareness from others' feedback and evaluations. However, if they excessively focus on others' thoughts and evaluations, they will develop "other-orientation." This pattern causes individuals to lose themselves, ignore their own needs and feelings, and ultimately leads to self-destruction and self-punishment.

## Difference from self-centeredness
"Other-orientation" and self-centeredness are two different psychological patterns. Self-centeredness refers to an individual's excessive focus on their own needs and feelings, ignoring others' thoughts and evaluations. In contrast, "other-orientation" refers to an individual's excessive focus on others' thoughts and evaluations, ignoring their own needs and feelings. Although both may lead to negative emotions and behaviors, their underlying causes and manifestations differ.

## Conclusion
"Other-orientation" is a common psychological pattern that makes individuals lose themselves, ignore their own needs and feelings, and ultimately leads to self-destruction and self-punishment. Understanding and changing "other-orientation" is crucial for an individual's mental health and self-growth. By practicing self-reflection and self-acceptance, individuals can gently recall their "other-oriented" tendencies, restore their sense of self, and achieve self-growth.
