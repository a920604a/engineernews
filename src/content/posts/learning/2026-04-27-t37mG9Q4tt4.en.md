# TL;DR

This article will teach you how to cope with breathing difficulties caused by psychological stress when an airplane cabin door closes, providing three emergency rescue methods to help you land safely.

# Prerequisites

* Basic knowledge of psychology
* Understanding of physiological changes during stress responses

# Steps

### Step 1: Understanding Your Body's Alarm System

When the airplane cabin door closes, your body undergoes physiological changes due to psychological stress, such as rapid heartbeat and shallow breathing. These changes are a manifestation of your body's alarm system going haywire, indicating that your brain is processing stress and fear. Recognizing these changes is the first step.

### Step 2: Using the Three Emergency Rescue Methods

#### 1. Deep Breathing

Deep breathing can help you relax your body and slow down your heart rate. Try inhaling through your nose and exhaling slowly through your mouth. Repeat this process until you feel your body start to relax.

#### 2. Body Relaxation

A tense body can make breathing difficult. Try relaxing your body, starting from your head and gradually moving down to your toes. This can help reduce muscle tension and improve breathing.

#### 3. Mindfulness Techniques

Mindfulness techniques can help you focus on the present moment, reducing worries about the future or past. Try focusing on your breath, feeling the air enter and leave your body. When you feel your heart rate increase or breathing quicken, try using mindfulness techniques to calm them down.

### Step 3: Combining the Three Emergency Rescue Methods

Once you're familiar with the three emergency rescue methods, try combining them. For example, you can start with deep breathing, followed by body relaxation, and finally use mindfulness techniques to calm your heart rate and breathing.

# Complete Example

Here's a simple mindfulness exercise you can use when the airplane cabin door closes:
```
I am currently on a plane, and I feel anxious and scared.
I will use deep breathing to relax my body.
I will focus on my breath, feeling the air enter and leave my body.
I will use mindfulness techniques to calm my heart rate and breathing.
```

# Common Pitfalls

* If you experience symptoms like rapid heartbeat, shallow breathing, or sweating, you may be experiencing a stress response.
* If you feel like you're focusing on the present moment, reducing worries about the future or past, you may be using mindfulness techniques effectively.
